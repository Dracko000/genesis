# Experiment Protocols

All experiments are implemented as pure functions in
`genesis/evaluation/experiments.py`. Each protocol below states the research
question, falsifiable hypothesis, variables, splits, baselines, metrics, seeds,
declared success criterion and failure conditions. Criteria were fixed before
the recorded runs.

Generated results:

- `experiments/E001_emergent_structure/results_seeds_0-1-2.json`
- `experiments/E002_structure_reuse/results_seeds_0-1-2.json`
- `experiments/E003_representation_revision/results_seeds_0-1-2.json`
- `experiments/E004_hybrid_ablation/results_seeds_0-1-2.json`

Reproduce:

```bash
py scripts/run_experiment.py E001
py scripts/run_experiment.py E002
py scripts/run_experiment.py E003
py scripts/run_experiment.py HYBRID
```

## E001 — Emergent structure

**Research question.** Can a learner form representations that improve prediction
on unseen feature combinations without being given high-level concepts?

**Falsifiable hypothesis.** A neural learner on raw neutral features achieves
held-out MSE at least 10% lower than nearest-neighbour and mean R² ≥ 0.5.

**Independent variable.** Learner type (neural, nearest-neighbour, ridge, mean,
oracle) and training-set size (50, 100, 200) for the sample-efficiency curve.

**Controlled variables.** Same world, rule (`interaction`), feature names,
split seed, held-out combination, noise level, and target definition.

**Data.** `SyntheticWorld.generate_compositional_split` with `n_train=400`,
`n_test=200`, held-out sign combination `(+,+)` for features `f1,f2`. Targets are
generated by `y = 1.2*f1*f2 + 0.6*f3`. Training contains no samples from the
held-out quadrant; test contains only that quadrant.

**Baselines.** Mean, 1-NN, ridge, oracle (evaluation only).

**Metrics.** MSE, MAE, R² on held-out combinations; sample-efficiency curve;
performance over seeds 0,1,2; noise sensitivity at σ ∈ {0.0, 0.1, 0.3}; feature
permutation probe.

**Seeds.** 0, 1, 2. Neural: `hidden=[32,32]`, 300 epochs, Adam lr 0.01, batch 32,
weight decay 1e-4.

**Expected outcome.** Neural beats memorisation and approaches the oracle on the
interaction rule; ridge fails because the rule is multiplicative.

**Declared success criterion.** Neural mean held-out MSE ≥10% lower than 1-NN
and neural mean R² ≥ 0.5.

**Failure conditions.** Criterion not met; or gains vanish under any seed; or a
target column is found in inputs; or the oracle is used by a learner.

**Recorded result (2026-10-29).** Criterion met: 81.7% lower MSE than 1-NN,
R² 0.788. Neural MSE 0.0405 ± 0.0091 vs 1-NN 0.2210 ± 0.0245, ridge 1.1280,
mean 0.3465, oracle 0.0. Feature permutation raised neural MSE to 0.1031.
Under σ=0.3, neural (0.5862) was worse than 1-NN (0.3572). Prediction gains are
not a concept-formation claim.

## E002 — Structure reuse

**Research question.** Does experience from one task reduce the amount of
learning needed for a related task?

**Falsifiable hypothesis.** Warm-start transfer from a related rule lowers
target-task error at equal target-task budget relative to fresh initialisation,
by at least 10%.

**Independent variable.** Initialisation/prior: (1) transfer from a `linear`
source, (2) fresh initialisation, (3) unrelated `quadratic` prior.

**Controlled variables.** Same target rule (`interaction`), target train/test
data, target budget (25 epochs for small-budget conditions), evaluation split,
and neural configuration.

**Data.** Source: 500 samples from `linear`; target: 120 train / 300 test from
`interaction`; source test for retention. Seeds 0,1,2.

**Baselines.** Equal-budget fresh initialisation; full-budget fresh (250 epochs);
unrelated prior; source-only (no target adaptation).

**Metrics.** Target MSE/MAE/R²; source retention MSE after adaptation.

**Expected outcome.** If reusable structure exists, transfer should reach lower
target error than fresh at the same target budget.

**Declared success criterion.** Transfer target MSE ≥10% lower than equal-budget
fresh initialisation.

**Failure conditions.** Criterion not met; or source retention collapses without
being reported; or target data leaks into source training.

**Recorded result (2026-10-29). NEGATIVE.** Transfer 0.010987 ± 0.006544 vs
fresh 0.003650 ± 0.000890 (transfer 201% worse). Unrelated prior 0.002547.
Source retention deteriorated from 0.000017 to 0.632762. The hypothesis was not
supported for this rule pair. This negative result is retained.

**Limitations.** One rule pair and one budget schedule; target-statistic
anchoring and output-layer commitment are plausible confounds. A follow-up
should test encoder-only transfer, target-statistic reset and warm-up schedules.

## E003 — Representation revision

**Research question.** Can the learner adapt when the underlying rule changes
while preserving performance on earlier tasks?

**Falsifiable hypothesis.** A validation-gated update reduces new-task MSE by at
least 10% after a rule change, and an isolated anomalous observation is not
accepted on its own.

**Independent variable.** Rule change (`interaction` → `changed`), revision
budget (5, 10, 25, 50 epochs), and update composition (clean, single anomaly,
contaminated batch, replay).

**Controlled variables.** Same features, split sizes, seeds and neural config.

**Data.** Stable task: 400 train / 80 validation / 300 test from `interaction`.
Changed task: 120 train / 100 validation / 300 test from `changed`
(`y = -0.7*f1 + 0.9*f2*f3 + 0.1`). Seeds 0,1,2.

**Procedure.** (1) train stable; (2) evaluate old and new test; (3) validation-
gated warm-start update on new train; (4) re-evaluate both tasks; (5) repeat with
an isolated anomaly, a contaminated batch, and a replay-consolidation variant.

**Metrics.** New-task MSE before/after; old-task MSE before/after; acceptance
rate; single-anomaly acceptance; adaptation-speed curve; replay variant.

**Expected outcome.** New-task error falls; validation rejects the isolated
anomaly; retention is measured rather than assumed.

**Declared success criterion.** New-task MSE improves ≥10% and isolated-anomaly
acceptance rate is 0.

**Failure conditions.** Criterion not met; or rollback does not restore exactly;
or validation data leaks into training; or retention is omitted from the report.

**Recorded result (2026-10-29).** Criterion met. New-task MSE 0.535701 →
0.002354 (99.6% improvement); anomaly acceptance 0/3. Retention was poor:
old-task MSE 0.000326 → 0.547721. Replay consolidation improved old-task MSE to
0.038171 but left new-task MSE at 0.342718. Retention was reported, not gated.

**Limitations.** One rule pair, low dimension, single-anomaly probe. Replay is an
exploratory variant and was not tuned.

## Hybrid ablation

**Research question.** Does a hybrid of the neural baseline and a low-level
symbolic basis improve held-out prediction relative to either alone?

**Falsifiable hypothesis.** A validation-selected hybrid is no worse than the
better single method by more than 5% on held-out data.

**Independent variables.** Neural-only, symbolic-basis-only, validation-selected
hybrid.

**Controlled variables.** Same data (`interaction`), train/validation/test split
(400/200/400), seeds 0,1,2.

**Metrics.** Held-out MSE/MAE/R²; validation errors; selected model per seed;
symbolic expression.

**Declared success criterion.** Hybrid held-out MSE ≤ 1.05 × the better single
method's MSE.

**Failure conditions.** Criterion not met; or the symbolic basis secretly
contains rule-specific high-level concepts; or budgets are not reported.

**Recorded result (2026-10-29).** Criterion met trivially: the symbolic basis
contains the true terms (`f1*f2`, `f3`), reaches machine-precision MSE, and was
selected in all seeds. Neural-only MSE was 0.000649. This is **confounded** and
does not demonstrate general hybrid superiority. The basis is a fixed prior and
is reported as such.
